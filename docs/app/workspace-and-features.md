# Workspace and features

Read this file only for work involving the GUI, navigation, an editor/viewer, or chapter-level behavior.

## Workspace

`fe_modding/gui/app.py` shows the launcher (new project, open folder, recent projects), then the project workspace (`gui/shell.py`). There is no permanent sidebar:

- **Top bar** — the six sections (Home, Chapters, Characters, Game Data, Assets, Tools), **Search everything… Ctrl+K**, the 💿 button (Disc & Patch) and the ⚙ **Settings** button (also **Settings ▸ Preferences…** in the menu bar): the light/dark theme and the **Emulator** box, Dolphin's location (found by itself in Program Files, `%LOCALAPPDATA%` and the user folder; a portable Dolphin elsewhere needs it), both remembered in `~/.tellius_modding/settings.json`.
- **Breadcrumbs** under it (Chapters › Ch.3: Pirates Aground › Deployment) with back/forward (**Alt+←/→**).
- **Status bar** — the editors holding unsaved edits, and the count of changes this session; clicking it opens the change log. The log records saves and imports made during the session; it is not persisted with the project.

Both games use the same pages and controls; where Radiant Dawn differs, the page shows its own fields on the same form. `app.md` lists what still differs, and [the parity test plan](parity-test-plan.md) what is still to be tried in game.

Pages:

- **Home** — the ways in (a tile per section), "Jump back in" (recent chapter and character pages), and this session's changes. Before extraction only Home and Disc & Patch are available, and Home shows only the Disc & Patch tile.
- **Game Code** (under Tools) — patches and tunable constants written straight into the extracted `sys/main.dol` (`fe_modding/game_code/`), for the US (`GFEE01`), PAL (`GFEP01`) and JP (`GFEJ01`) builds of Path of Radiance and Radiant Dawn's US build (`RFEE01`, the groups both games share under the same names and grids; see `app.md`): combat rules (critical damage multiplier, double-attack speed gap, Wrath bonus, weapon-effectiveness multiplier, single-roll hit chance, no crits, class critical bonus, no terrain bonus, Charisma bonus and range, Horror penalty and range, support bonus range, doubled support bonuses, stealing from faster units, weapon triangle and magic trinity Hit/Atk for advantage and disadvantage), gold and forge (gold cap, forging more than once per chapter, Hit and Crit per forge step, class rings on the first playthrough), promotion (Ike and thieves promote normally: their classes carry SID_EVENTCC, which otherwise stops their EXP at level 20 and refuses the Master Seal), supports (points per chapter, star limit), random numbers (same random numbers every boot, seed), a multiplier and bonus for the activation chance of 15 skills, EXP and growth (Elite and Frac90 factors, EXP cap, promoted-level bonus, forced fixed/random growth mode, gain per stat in each mode, fixed-growth threshold), Laguz transformation gauge (gain/drain per turn and when struck, size), map options (fog of war on/off, bandits not burning villages, unlimited or limited reinforcement uses, free and smooth camera rotation), developer tools (debug menus in the pause menu, development title menu, performance/process-tree overlays always on), animation options (animation speed multiplier, animated ally/NPC battles) and the battle screen (each fighter's damage, hit and critical rate printed under their HP window during battle animations, placed under the HP windows, above them or at the bottom of the screen, with position nudges and text size). The chapters' story-flow table lives in the same file but is edited from the Chapters pages. The build is identified by the executable's hash (or the disc code once edited); an unknown build is shown read-only, and every site is checked against its expected original bytes before it is written. Changes save at once and are listed in the session's changes; "Reset everything" restores every catalog entry (the Sound Room track list, edited on Assets > Soundroom Tracks, is kept). `python -m fe_modding.game_code.verify <main.dol>` checks the catalog against a real executable.
- **Disc & Patch** — **Export as text…** (under Version control; `text_export.py`; Radiant Dawn exports its FE10Data and FE10Growth tables, deployments, shops, messages, scripts and sound cues) writes the game data as text for git and diffs: the FE8Data tables (JSON), every deployment file, shops, messages (`## ID` then the text, in the game's characters), decompiled scripts (`.fe9s`), music and sound-effect cues, the face table and every DBX table. Export only. It refuses a non-empty folder that holds no earlier export (marker file `.tellius_text_export`) and replaces its own folders on each run. Then: source disc (choose, extract, re-extract with a warning), **Build disc**, **Build and play in Dolphin** (builds, then starts the image with `--exec` and `--batch`; `emulator.py`), **Mod details** (name, author, version and credits, written into patches), **Create patch…** and **Apply patch…** (see [Project lifecycle](project-lifecycle.md#patches)). **File ▸ Apply patch to a disc…** opens the same apply dialog without a project.
- **Chapters** — the **Add chapter…**, **New campaign…** and **Story order…** cards at the top, then a card per chapter with the game's own title (`MCTnn` in `mess/common.m`), message and unit counts. Chapters with numbered playable openings show a **parts** chip; other chapters with several map folders show a **map phases** chip. Cards sit under **Story** in the order a new game plays them (numbered #1, #2…, following the story flow), or under **Other chapters** when no chapter leads to them. The three top cards are **Add chapter…**, **New campaign…** (several chapters in story order and several characters in one step, `campaign.py`; **Empty** removes the copied units except the player's army, the `mikata` sections) and **Story order…** (the whole order at once) (see [Chapters](#chapters) and [Making a new campaign](modder-guide.md)).
- **Chapter page** — title, disc number and files, previous/next chapter, and a selector for map folders (`bmap06`, `bmap06_2`…). A folder loaded by a numbered `Opening18_N` script function is labeled **Part N**; other folders are labeled **Map phase N**. The adjacent Part or Phase buttons move within the chapter. The selection drives the Build, Map settings, Chapter data and Battle scenes tabs and the Overview cast. Tabs: **Overview** (the **Story flow**: the chapters played before it and a **Next chapter** choice, see [Chapters](#chapters); the cast of the chosen deployment file grouped by section, each unit opening its character page; the messages grouped by ID prefix, each opening the Dialogue tab on it; the named or triggered script functions, each opening the Script tab on it), **Build**, **Deployment**, **Dialogue**, **Script**, **Shops**.
- **Characters** — a portrait card per character, filtered as Playable, Named NPCs & bosses, Generic or All (search covers every category). Story copies sharing a name key (`PID_BOLE_MAP1`) are grouped under the main record. Radiant Dawn shows the same page from `FE10Data.cms`.
- **Character page** — portrait, name, class → promoted class (links to Game Data), and the copies of the record. Tabs: **Stats** (the FE8Data record: identity with the name key (`MPID`), skills, personal weapon ranks, map animation IDs, level/build/weight, transform gauge, roster order, biorhythm pattern and phase, personal bonus and growth next to the class's values, the fixed-growth start accumulators, and the whole record as hex; **New character…** adds a record, empty or a copy of one, and **Remove** drops this one), **Portrait & models** (face file, and the battle and map models the game resolves for the character, unpromoted and promoted, each opening 3D Models), **Appearances** (every deployment record and every chapter message that loads the portrait or names the character; double-click opens it), **Script references** (functions pushing the `PID_`/`MPID_`).
- **Game Data** — tabs for classes, items, skills, terrain types, chapters, battle scenes, the general tables, **Supports**, **Map Objects** and **AI (CP)** (`cp_data.bin`, see [AI scripts](ai-scripts.md)). There is no side list: each tab picks its record with a bar at the top (a search box whose drop-down narrows to the entries matching every typed word, Enter picks the first match; ◀ ▶ or Page Up/Page Down step through the table), and the record's form fills the page. Every field applies as soon as it is left (Tab, Enter, a click elsewhere, a drop-down choice); an invalid value is reported next to **Save FE8Data.bin** and not written. **Discard unsaved edits** reloads the file for every view of it (character pages included). Supports edit the same shared `FE8Data.bin` as the rest of the page; see [Formats and tools](formats-and-tools.md).
- **Flags** (a Game Data tab) — the 96 script flags (`formats/event_flags.py`), in three tabs. **Campaign flags**: the engine's eight `gf_*` flags and `startup.cmb`'s `RegistGlobalFlags` list, each with its slot and meaning; **Add campaign flag…** appends one, **Rename reserved slot…** renames one of the three unused `G_リザーブド` entries, and a line gives the busiest chapter's remaining room (scanned in the background). **Chapter flags**: a chapter's registrations from `Startup` with their slots, the function registering each and every function that sets, clears or tests it (or uses it as a talk/battle/base-conversation label); **Add chapter flag…** appends one after the last registration. Both rewrite the script at once (compiled onto the current `.cmb`, source kept in `script_sources/`), refuse names that are taken or not Shift-JIS, and leave alone a file with unsaved edits in the Script tab. **Save file**: opens a `.gci`, lists a save block's 96 flags by name (chapter flags named from the script of the block's map) and switches one per double-click or Space; **Save** / **Save as…** write it with the block's checksum recomputed.
- **Assets** — a tile per resource type, each opening its viewer full-page.
- **Tools** — **Battle Simulator** (attacker above defender with compact stat boxes, forecast, outcome, rolls and fight log on the right; each unit's level can be raised and an unpromoted unit promoted (at a chosen level) into its linked class, the stats then following the game's fixed growth (bases, signed personal bonuses, growth rates plus class modifiers, caps, counters halved on promotion); distance and battle scenery under the units; **Render fight** opens a window that plays the fight in real time as the game stages it: the engine's start spots, runs, waits and clip choices, the `xcam/` camera with its tweens, mirroring and shake, a 30° 4:3 view and the backdrop placed by its `map.bin`; the game's effects where it spawns them, with their particle emitters drawn as camera-facing cards (an approximation of the game's particle draw; colour curves not applied): hit sparks, miss and no-damage marks, spells travelling from the caster to the target and fading by their material alpha tracks, skills, death and the backdrop's dust, splashes or snow; nothing to pick by hand), **Tutorials** and **Staff Credits** are under Assets: the in-game tutorial list (`tut_data_en.bin`: title key with its text, script and function, chapter/category/initial group, list order, unlocked/learned start flags per difficulty) and the end credits (`ending/staffroll_noa.bin`, three columns per line). **Saves** (the `.gci` editor; **Play in Dolphin…** tests the selected file-save block, unsaved edits included, either as saved or moved to the start of any chapter with its army: a copy of the save goes alone into a GCI folder in `build/dolphin_run/card_a`, Dolphin's slot A is pointed at it for that run only (`--config` overrides, Dolphin's settings are not changed) and the project's own `main.dol` boots normally, so the file is picked in the game's file menu; the original `.gci` is never written), **Check** and **Game Code**. **Check** (`validator.py`) lists broken `PID_`/`JID_`/`IID_`/`SID_` references, duplicate IDs, missing chapter files, deployments and shops naming missing records, script strings naming them (warnings) and added chapters the story flow never reaches; a missing ID in a deployment file is one row per file. Double-click opens the character, class, skill, supports or chapter page. An untouched retail extraction has no errors and 4 warnings from the retail data itself.

The character page and Game Data edit one shared copy of `FE8Data.bin` (`gui/fe8_session.py`), so neither overwrites the other; saving from either writes it once.

Editors are created when first opened and stay alive for the project session, so moving between pages never loses edits. Opening another chapter asks before discarding that chapter's unsaved edits; closing the project, and building, list the editors with unsaved edits.

## Chapters

`fe_modding/chapters.py` discovers chapter resources and groups dialogue, deployment, script, and map data under one chapter selection. Its IDs are disc file numbers, which are also the game's chapter numbers (`ChapterData` ids): the Prologue is `01`, so these numbers do not match the chapter numbers displayed to players; the pages show the game's titles instead. `chapter_phases()` lists a chapter's map folders.

### Chapter data objectives

On a Path of Radiance chapter's **Chapter data** tab, the Normal objective text keys use four slots in the `ChapterData` record:

| Normal slot | Label | Text key prefix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Goal | `MW_` |
| 1 | Goal second line | `MW_` |
| 2 | Defeat condition | `ML_` |
| 3 | Defeat second line | `ML_` |

Hard and Maniac each have three override fields. Their fields 0, 1 and 2 override Normal slots 0, 1 and 3 respectively. The defeat condition in Normal slot 2 is shared by all three difficulties. An empty override uses the Normal text. Select an existing text key from the dropdown or create a new one there; save these changes with **Save FE8Data.bin**.

The music and background fields link to the selected cue or resource in Assets › Music and Assets › Backgrounds.

### Adding a chapter

**Add chapter…** (Path of Radiance) creates a playable chapter from a template chapter (`chapters.add_story_chapter`):

- **Files** — the template's dialogue, script and every map folder (`bmap09`, `bmap09_2`…) are copied under the new number. The deployments' section names and the script's own `bmapNN` strings (`MapLoad("bmap09")`, `DisposFirst("bmap09_mikata_c")`) are renamed to the new number; another chapter's map named by the script (Ch.8 loads `bmap08`) is left alone.
- **Data** — a `ChapterData` record copied from the template's, with the new id and the new map, script, message and title names (objectives, music and backgrounds stay the template's: edit them in the chapter's Chapter data tab); a battle-scene row for the new map; the three base-shop sections in each difficulty's shop file; the title as `MCTnn` in the loose `Mess/common.m` (copied into `system.cmp` at build). Two pictures of the name are drawn from the title with the game's large serif font, on copies of the template's: the file menu's save-slot strip (`window/chapter2/chapNN.cms`) and the title card at the chapter start (`window/chapter/chapNN.cms`); replace them like any other image. A build draws any that are missing for an added chapter. Adding a chapter saves `FE8Data.bin`, after asking when it has unsaved edits.
- **Number** — chosen among the free numbers 33–89: 0–31 are the retail story, 32 plays the ending, 51/52/58 and 80–82 are other records and 90 up are trial maps.
- **Story flow** — **Played after** puts the chapter in the story: that chapter now leads to the new one, which leads to where it used to go. **Not in the story** creates it unreached.

The story flow is a table patched into `sys/main.dol` (`game_code/chapter_flow.py`, US/PAL/JP): when a chapter is cleared the game goes to the table's entry for it instead of `+ 1`, and the save menu accepts an added chapter that is in the table (retail refuses every chapter above 31). Each chapter page's **Story flow** shows what leads to it and sets its **Next chapter** (any chapter, or the ending); **Take out of the story** routes around an added chapter. With no entries left the patch is removed and `main.dol` is retail again. The table is not listed on the Game Code page and **Reset everything** there leaves it.

Limits: chapters 28 and 29 are the two halves of Ch.27 (no save menu and no army cleanup between them, whatever follows 28); the save's clear history holds 40 chapters (retail uses 31), so the war record of a longer story loses its last chapters; a copied script keeps its template's flags and message IDs (its `ChapterMoviePlay(N)`, which draws title card `window/chapter/chapN.cms` over `movie/chapter.thp`, is renumbered to the new chapter; a full-screen movie of its own is a `MoviePlay("Movie/<file>.thp")` call with a video added on Assets › Videos). Not yet play-tested in Dolphin.

## Chapter tools

- **Dialogue** — edits chapter messages and shows a playable in-app preview of the selected message. The message is chosen in the **Message ID** box at the top (type an ID or part of it and press Enter, or open the list, which shows each ID with the start of its text); **New…**, **Duplicate**, **Rename…** and **Delete** beside it manage messages, and new ones can start from a template. For Path of Radiance the left column lists the message's steps in its top half, with a **Step** tab (the selected step's fields) and a **Raw text** tab below; the right column shows the stage on top and the preview in the rest. Both edit the same bytes. The preview decodes background changes, portrait slots, speaker names, waits/page breaks, and textbox text reveal over time when the referenced assets can be resolved. Portraits are composited from the base face plus eye/mouth pieces so normal animated portrait archives do not show transparent holes.
- **Build** — builds the map on one top-down canvas: a render of the terrain and props seen from above (redrawn about a second after a prop edit), the tile grid, the playable-area frame, the props (outlined over the tiles they cover) and the units of one difficulty file (a disc coloured by army with the character's first letters; a dashed arrow leads to the tile the unit walks to after appearing). Layers can be shown or hidden (map image, terrain colours, heights - each tile shaded by its combined height, red highest - grid, props, units), and the units can be limited to one section. The status line shows the hovered tile's terrain and corner heights. Ctrl+wheel zooms, middle-drag pans. Tools:
  - **Select / move** — click a unit or a prop to edit it in the right panel, drag it to another tile; click any other tile to edit its heights. **R** turns the selected prop a quarter turn, **Delete** removes it; right-click offers the same, and **Tile heights...**.
  - **Paint terrain** — click or drag to paint a terrain type, or fill a rectangle; right-click picks a tile's type.
  - **Place prop** — click a tile to place the chosen prop at the chosen angle, at the ground height there. A moved prop keeps its height above the ground. **Add from another map...** lists the props of every other chapter map (the Map Objects browser, with its 3D preview); **Add to this map** (or a double-click) copies the chosen one in (its model, skeleton, animation, `texpack.tpl` images, flags and effect) and chooses it for Place prop. A prop of the same name that is the same model is just chosen; one that differs is copied under a name you give (suggested: `<name>_2`). The copy is undone like any other Build edit and written by **Save Chapter**.
  - **Tile heights** — click a tile to edit the four corner elevations (top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right) of each of its panel layers: **Combined** (what units and the cursor stand on), **Ground** (the land under objects) and **Roof** (buildings, on maps that have it). Values are the game's: -400 is one unit higher (a tile is 5 units wide), and two neighbouring corners 400 or more apart close the way between their tiles. Tiles share panel records, so an edited tile gets a record of its own and keeps its terrain type. **Deduce from terrain and props** estimates them: the ground is the terrain surface (or the deck of a low prop over it, such as a bridge), and the combined and roof layers are the ground, or flat at the height vanilla gives the props covering the tile (trees 7.19 units above their base, walls 4, houses about 6.3; a prop vanilla doesn't have rises to its model's top, unless its flags mark it as one that never does, like thickets). It reproduces about 80% of vanilla tiles, so check the result in game. Drag to select a rectangle of tiles: **Deduce** them all, or set every corner of one layer (or all) to one value. With **Heights follow props** (on by default) placing, moving, turning or deleting a prop deduces the tiles it covered and now covers.
  - Section lists say what each section is for, from the retail naming and the chapter script: player army (`mikata`), battle start (`first`), reinforcements (`zoen`, `z01`…), boss, event scene only (units shown during a scene, not in the battle), or not deployed by the script. The battle's enemies are in the Normal/Hard/Maniac files; `dispos_c` (every difficulty) usually holds only the player army and scene units.
  - **Place unit** — click a tile, choose the character, class, level and section (the tool starts on the battle-start section; in a file without one, such as `dispos_c`, the dialog says where the battle's enemies are); the unit is added to the chosen section of every ticked difficulty file, with the AI, army and flags of the section's first unit.

  The unit panel (its **Apply**, **Delete**, **All fields...** and **View Stats...** buttons at the top) edits the character, class, army (player, enemy, ally, partner), spawn and destination tiles, eight items (each with its **drop** and class-**ring** flags), five skills, the four AI entries, the laguz gauge, the AI turn order (lower acts first) and every deployment flag as a checkbox (autolevel, hold position, group commander, spawn if absent, blocked by bridge cover, AI uses Door Key, AI uses Chest Key, and the unread bit 0x80 when a record sets it). A **Section** block below edits the unit's section: its army name (Crimean Army, Bandits… from `FE8Data.bin`'s `GroupData`), whether units may be placed on occupied tiles, and the mode byte; **Apply to section** writes it to the same section in every difficulty when **Same edit on every difficulty** is on. Any character, class, item or skill of `FE8Data.bin` can be used, including ones the chapter never had. The difficulty table below it has a row per file (`c` is deployed on every difficulty; `n`/`h`/`m` are picked by difficulty): tick or untick a file to add the unit to it or remove it, and set its level and eight stat bonuses there. With **Same edit on every difficulty** (on by default) a move, a deletion and the form's other fields also apply to the same unit in the other files (same section, same character). A section only appears in game if the chapter script deploys it (see the Script tab).

  The **Map** buttons at the bottom left open three windows on the map, which follow its edits:
  - **Objects...** — every placed object of the map (`mapbuildinst`, including the terrain and the scenery beyond the grid), searchable. Selecting one selects it on the canvas and the other way round. Its object, tile, angle (0°, 90°, 180°, 270° or a raw byte), height (the game's value; the model sits at -5 × height) and covered tiles are editable; **Apply** (or Enter) writes the changed ones. A move keeps the object's height above the ground and a turn recomputes the covered tiles; **Put on the ground** sets the height to the ground at its tile. **Add...** places an object, **Delete** removes one; **View model...** and **Export .glb...** show and save its model.
  - **3D view...** — the whole chapter in 3D (terrain and every placed prop), rebuilt a moment after each edit (**Update after each edit**), with the placement and gameplay grids.
  - **Map settings & chapter...** — the Settings tab's content in a window.

  Edits go to the map and the Deployment tab's data, and **Save Chapter** (here or in the Deployment tab) writes them; **Undo**/**Redo** (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y, also in the windows) cover every Build edit and window edit.

  **Play...** (next to Save Chapter) plays the chapter in the app (`fe_modding/playthrough/`, `gui/playthrough_window.py`): the map with the units of the current state, the event scripts, messages, the enemy AI (`cp_data.bin`) and combat, with a rewindable history. **Skip the opening** (on by default) plays straight to the first player turn; the messages stay in the log and the history. Click one of your units, then a tile, and pick an action (attacks show the forecast). The transport bar steps by line, message page, action, AI unit, phase or until your turn; the line under it says what is running. The side panel shows the unit, the running script and AI (with **how the target was chosen**: every possible attack, best first, scored like the game's `ai_score_attack_tile_mtype_*` from the unit's MTYPE weights - expected damage, % of the foe's HP, class bonuses, turn, counter damage and own HP lost, terrain; adjacent foes and the skill term are not simulated; after the unit acts, its last decision stays shown), the message (drawn like the Conversations preview, text appearing over time), the log and the flags. Script instructions share one history entry unless stepping line by line. Limits: fog is class vision only, Preparations only chooses among placed units, a choice dialog takes the first answer unless **Choices...** plans others, and `MapLoad` and cameras are skipped.
- **Settings** — the phase map's settings and the chapter's Game Data record on one page. **Map**: capacity scale, lighting (model and light colours), fog (on/off, effect type, transparency, colour), grid colour, and the `mapextra` record with the **grid border** (the tiles around the map edge outside the playable grid; the playable grid size is shown above) and the map flags; each colour has a swatch and a **Pick...** colour chooser (which applies at once). **Apply** puts the changes on the Build tab's Undo/Redo and **Save Chapter** writes them; **Import water .glb...** and **Water flow...** edit the water. **Chapter**: the chapter's record in Game Data › Chapters (`FE8Data.bin`: title, objectives, music, backgrounds, enemy levels, trial grades, the whole record in hex), the same form and session as Game Data, saved with **Save FE8Data.bin**; **Open in Game Data ›** jumps there. A map used by several records (trial maps) offers a record picker.
- **Deployment** — lists every section (with its army) and unit of each difficulty file with its 48 fields, shown decoded (`0x4C (Group commander, …)`, `1 (Enemy)`, `1 (drop)`). Double-clicking a field opens the editor its kind needs: any label (`PID_`, `JID_`, `IID_`…) for the 4-byte fields, a checkbox per bit with an explanation for the flags and item flags, a list for the army, and a number for the rest. **Edit Section...** (or double-clicking a section) edits the section header: army name, occupied-tile placement and mode. **Add Unit** duplicates the selected unit, **Delete Unit** removes it. Saving keeps the extracted `dispos.cmp` in `originals/`.
- **Script** — edits the chapter's event script as Python-style source ([Script language](script-language.md)): a **Function** drop-down to pick the function, a form for each function's name, export and trigger (type and parameters), a code editor with completion and error marks, an **Insert call…** picker for the game's functions, and a bytecode view. Saving compiles the whole script. The **File** box at the top right switches between the chapter's script and the shared ones: `startup.cmb` (the helpers every chapter calls and the campaign flags) and `ending.cmb`. A shared script stays open when another chapter is picked, and its unsaved edits aren't discarded by the switch; a link to one of the chapter's functions reopens the chapter's script. Compiling uses the project's own `startup.cmb`: its campaign flags and its exported helpers are known to every chapter script.
- **Shops** (Path of Radiance) — the chapter's base shops in `shop/shopitem_<n|h|m>.bin`, one file per difficulty picked with **Difficulty** (Easy uses the Normal file). **Armory** and **Vendor** are ordered item lists with each item's price (cost × uses from `FE8Data.bin`, unsaved edits included): **Add** inserts the item picked or typed in the box below the selected one, **Remove** (or Delete) and ▲ ▼ edit the list. **Forge** is the fixed grid of 5 rows (swords, lances, axes, bows, tomes) by 9 bases (Iron, Slim, Steel, Silver, Thrown, Fire, Thunder, Wind, Light); each cell names the weapon offered or **(none)**. **Copy to other difficulties** replaces the chapter's three shops in the other two files. A chapter without shops (an added chapter) gets **Add shops for chapter NN**, a copy of the closest lower chapter's in every file. The files hold every chapter's shops, so edits stay when another chapter is opened; **Save Shops** writes the changed files and keeps the extracted ones in `originals/`. Radiant Dawn's shop files use another layout and aren't editable.

## Global-data tools

- **Backgrounds and portraits** — view and replace supported textures. Replacing a texture that has mip levels (the smaller copies the game draws at a distance, used by map, battle-scenery and `etc/fire.tpl` textures) rewrites them from the new image too, on every texture page. The Backgrounds panel also shows the `s/rect.bin` resource that uses the selected file (its `RID_` name, layer count, cross-fade period, brightness and weather overlay, all editable and saved to `rect.bin`), and **Add New Background...** encodes one or more images as a new TPL under `files/s/` and registers a new resource; an event script then shows it with `RectBuild("RID_...")` (see `research/RECT_NOTES.md`).
- **Portraits** (`gui/portrait_viewer.py`, `fe_modding/portraits.py`) — every `Face/*.cms` texture set with the face IDs that use it. The pictures (body, mouth frames, eye frames, 64×64 menu face) are shown with the face **as the game assembles it**. **Replace picture…** with **New palette** (on by default) re-encodes the whole set with a fresh 256-colour palette per palette group (`portraits.rebuild_with_new_palette`, `tpl.build_c8_tpl`), so the new picture keeps its colours; off, the picture is matched to the current palette. The **Face record** box edits the selected face's `Face/facedata.bin` record (nameplate key `MPID_`, eye and mouth positions, which parts exist, menu-face texture, automatic mouth set, depth, lowering in menus); **Preview** redraws, **Save face record** writes the file (the build copies it into `system.cmp`). **New portrait…** builds a new pair from pictures: `FID_<NAME>` (the body fitted to 192×128, optional six mouth and six eye frames with their positions, a menu face cut from the body unless one is given) and `FID_<L_NAME>` (the large portrait, its own size, by default the small body enlarged), writes `Face/<NAME>.cms` and `Face/L_<NAME>.cms`, and can set a character's FID (saved with FE8Data.bin). - **3D Models** — views supported models, skeletons, and animations: map models (`ymu/<class>`), battle models (`zu/<code>`, all weapon packs of the folder as one set), battle scenery (`zbg/`) and weapons (`xwp/<name>`, and `xwp/forge/<name>_b` for forged weapons; Game Data's items link here). Shapes the game never draws (flag `0x400`, such as the weapon placeholders inside battle models) are left out of the preview and the export. **Export .glb...** saves the selected set (mesh, skeleton, textures, animations) for Blender and other glTF tools. **Import .glb...** replaces the set's mesh and repacks the `.cmp`; the skeleton and animations are kept. On battle scenery (`zbg/`) and weapons (`xwp/`) the model is imported as unskinned geometry and the set's `.tpl` is replaced when the model has textures (with a half-size mip level per image when the replaced file had them, as every vanilla `zbg/` texture pack does); a weapon keeps its skeleton and moves with its first bone, and bows are refused (their string bends through skinned shapes this import doesn't write). A material's glTF alpha mode (Blender's blend mode) sets how the game draws it: opaque, alpha clip (`MASK`, for cut-outs such as leaves; pixels under half alpha are dropped) or alpha blend (`BLEND`, translucent). Exported sets carry the mode each material has in the game, so keep it when editing. On a map model (`ymu/`) or a battle model (`zu/`) the model must be weighted to the set's armature (export the set first and weight to its bones). Vertices weighted to several bones (up to four) blend smoothly, as in the game's own battle models. A battle model is written into every weapon pack of its folder and built entirely from CPU-blended shapes, the only kind that draws correctly in battle. Its textures stay as they are when the glTF images come from one of the folder's `.tpl` files; new images replace every `.tpl` of the folder (256×256), so every unit using that battle model shows them. Do not model weapons into a battle body: the game draws the equipped weapon's own model, and a modelled weapon would appear next to it. On a map model, a texture identical to one of the class's `tex_N.tpl` variants keeps them all; a new texture is fitted to 128×128 and written to every variant, so all units of the class share it. **Import animation...** (map and battle models; a battle animation is rewritten in the weapon pack that holds it) replaces one of the set's animations with an animation from a glTF file; the replaced animation's events (hit frame, sounds) are kept unless the glTF carries its own. It warns when the new animation moves smoothly weighted vertices beyond the range the body stores; re-importing the body afterwards resizes it. **Replace rig...** builds a new skeleton from a glTF armature and rebuilds the body and **every** animation on it; the armature must keep the old rig's underscore-named anchor bones (`_s1_`, `_sw1_`, `_r_hand_`...). On a map model (`ymu/`) the glTF must contain an animation named like each of the class's animation files. On a battle model (`zu/`) every weapon pack is rewritten: an animation the glTF has under a file's name (`fig1_at1_ax`) is imported, every other one is moved from the old skeleton by bone name (tracks of bones the new rig lacks are dropped, positions follow the new proportions), and the review lists both counts. When fewer than half the old body bones are in the armature (a new skeleton with its own bone names), nothing can be moved by name, so the glTF must provide every animation: a missing follow-up clip (`fig1_cr1_ax_at1`) reuses its base clip (`fig1_cr1_ax`), a clip missing for one weapon reuses the same role of another (`fig1_dam_no` from `fig1_dam_ax`), `name@pack` (`fig1_dam_ax@ha`) fills one weapon pack only, and anything still missing refuses the import. Both rig imports refuse an animation that lacks an event code the fight waits for (`0` the blow lands and `1` the swing on melee attacks, `2` the release on throws) where the original has it; an animation without events of its own keeps the original's, retimed. A map rig is also checked for its weapons: each animation must show and hide the weapon bones (`Ax`, `Ax1` on the Fighter) as the original does, by scaling them to 1 or 0. **Rig kit...** writes what a replacement rig must provide: a `.glb` with the set's skeleton, body and every animation named as **Replace rig...** looks for them, a `.md` checklist (the anchor bones with their positions and purpose, the map weapons each animation shows, and every animation with its pack, frame count, loop, role from `zu/<code>.dbx` and events, the required codes in bold) and a `.json` of the animation list for scripts. **Fit new rig...** puts any rigged humanoid glTF (one texture) on a map or battle model with **its own skeleton**: it finds the feet, hands, head, pelvis and chest on both skeletons (from the game rig's anchor bones, else from the shape of the body), guesses which game bone each model joint follows, and shows that mapping for editing (blank: the joint follows its parent, for fingers or hair; **Save mapping...**/**Load mapping...** keep it as JSON). The model is then scaled to the game model's height and turned so each bone points like the game bone it follows; the game's anchor bones are added under the matching joints (on a battle model, on small carrier bones at the game offset) and, on a map model, the weapon bones with the game's own weapon meshes. Every animation of the set is retargeted: each joint turns as its game bone turns, the pelvis moves as the game's does, weapons are shown and hidden as before, and the events are the game's. The result goes through **Replace rig...** (and its review); the fitted glTF, with its skeleton and every animation, is also saved next to the chosen model as `<model>_on_<set>.glb`, for editing in Blender. A mesh weapon held by the model can be taken for its hand: check the arm lines of the mapping. On a map model (`ymu/`), **Import .glb...**, **Replace rig...** and **Fit new rig...** offer to reduce a model with more triangles than any vanilla map model (853; the median is 444). You choose the triangle count (suggested: the current body's, or 500). An object's flag bytes and an instance's covered-tiles rectangle (`x2`..`end_y2`) are editable in the Objects and Instances tabs; the rectangle matters only for roofs, houses, doors and rocks (flag byte c bit 3 or 4). The import reports tiles under the water that are not a water terrain type, and water-type tiles left dry; paint them with the Build tab's **Paint terrain** tool.

Terrain types are painted with the Build tab's **Paint terrain** tool: the list shows each type's English name, Japanese name, and its avoid/defence/resistance, healing, or "impassable"; hovering a tile shows its type. The yellow frame is the playable area. A type such as Fort (`砦`) is an impassable building in this game; the healing tile is Heal (`回復`). **Save Chapter** keeps the extracted `map.cmp` in `originals/`, so **Restore original...** in the 3D Models panel (`zmap/<map>`) undoes map edits.

Pages link related records: a deployment unit's **View Stats...** and every cast card open the character page, which links to its class, portrait, models, chapters, messages and script functions.

## Scope limitations

The feature set reflects the formats currently understood by the project. A visible record or raw field is not necessarily editable. New labels, shared data, executable-controlled behavior, and incompletely understood fields may remain read-only to avoid unsafe changes.

The FE9 dialogue preview uses real face-table coordinates, portrait depth, window descriptors and game glyph metrics. Auto-advance defaults on; selecting another message resets to its first frame, and playback highlights its current source position in the editor. Unambiguous inherited scene context is recovered from direct chapter TalkEvent branches. The editable **Inherit from message** field shows the detected predecessor: choose a chapter message or type its ID and press Enter/Apply to use its final scene as the preview's initial context. Clear it to remove inheritance; **Auto** restores script-derived context. These overrides affect only the preview and reset when selecting another message. The expandable context panel also supports individual property adjustments. It renders the selected message, not the 3D map/event VM. Animation timing, some formatting and nonstandard descriptors still have limits documented in [Conversation rendering](../../research/CONVERSATION_RENDERING.md). FE10 retains its earlier, unvalidated preview.

### FE9 scene editor

The step list shows the selected message as steps: layout, background, transition duration and fades; portrait load/remove, eyes, blink speed, mouth set and seat selection; box speaker, select, dismiss, window visibility and nameplate; lines of text; wait for input; yield to script; and the recognised but undecoded controls (`$UB`, `$Ub`, `$SD`, `$SE`, `$G`, `$Y`, `$C`, `$O<n>`). Anything else becomes a Raw step that keeps its bytes. Every vanilla message decompiles without Raw steps, and untouched steps keep their exact source.

- The pinned **Starting scene** row shows what the message inherits: the preview's resolved or overridden initial context.
- **Add step** inserts after the selection. Steps can be duplicated, deleted and moved (also Delete and Alt+Up/Down in the list). Selecting a step moves the preview to the scene after it, and playback highlights the playing step.
- The step form edits the selected step's fields. Portraits and backgrounds have searchable pickers with previews. A Line has a speaker target (`$F<n>`, `$s<n>` or `$W<n>`), "clear box first" (`$P`), "wait for input" (`$K`), and a text box where inline commands (`$w<n>`, `$MC…$MD`, `$N`, `#C`, `#F`, …) are highlighted and described on hover. An **Insert** menu adds common ones. A **Font** menu draws the selected text in another font: the Tellius alphabet of the ancient-language lines (`#F01$O2…#F02$O1`, which also switches the typing sound as vanilla does), system, bigkana or alpha (`#F00`/`#F03`/`#F04` … `#F02`). A font switch lasts one textbox line, so each selected line gets its own codes; with nothing selected the pair is inserted at the cursor, and **Remove font codes** strips them. The preview warns when the text uses a character the chosen font lacks (the game draws ☆, or a space in the fonts without one).
- The **stage** shows every seat of the current layout after the selected step, with the speaker highlighted. Click an empty seat to load a portrait. Right-click a portrait to replace or remove it, make it speak, or change its eyes, blinking or mouth set. Layouts with at most five seats (上下会話, 背景上下会話, TUT会話, ダイアログ会話) use box commands (`$c`/`$d`/`$s`); larger ones use seat commands (`$F`).

Message IDs are listed and saved in ascending byte order, like every vanilla file. The game itself looks IDs up through a hash, so order does not matter to it. A new, duplicated or renamed ID is rejected if it already exists in the chapter or `common.m`, or if it shares the engine hash key with one of those IDs. Renaming or deleting warns when the chapter script (`.cmb` or its `.fe9s` source) still references the ID; scripts are never edited automatically.
